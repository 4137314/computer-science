#+title: Power dissipation in CMOS
#+summary: \(P=\alpha\,C_L V_{DD}^2 f+I_{sc}V_{DD}+I_{leak}V_{DD}\): dynamic switching power (activity \(\alpha\), switched capacitance, the square of the supply, frequency), short-circuit power during transitions, and static leakage; the quadratic dependence on \(V_{DD}\) makes the supply the strongest lever.

* Model
- *Switching (dynamic) power*: every \(0\to1\) transition at a node of capacitance \(C_L\) draws \(C_LV_{DD}^2\) from the supply (half stored, half burned in the pull-up; the stored half is burned at the \(1\to0\) transition). With \(\alpha\) = probability of a \(0\to1\) transition per cycle:
\[ P_{dyn}=\alpha\,C_L\,V_{DD}^2\,f. \]
- *Short-circuit power*: during a slow input transition both networks conduct briefly; it grows with input slew, typically 10–15% of the dynamic power.
- *Static power*: subthreshold and gate leakage, \(P_{stat}=I_{leak}V_{DD}\); it flows even when nothing switches and grows exponentially as the threshold voltage drops.

* Derivation
Charging \(C_L\) from 0 to \(V_{DD}\) moves charge \(Q=C_LV_{DD}\) out of the supply at voltage \(V_{DD}\): energy \(QV_{DD}=C_LV_{DD}^2\). The capacitor ends with \(\tfrac12C_LV_{DD}^2\), so the other half is dissipated in the PMOS. Discharging dissipates the stored half in the NMOS. One full cycle \(0\to1\to0\) costs \(C_LV_{DD}^2\); at \(\alpha f\) such cycles per second, \(P=\alpha C_LV_{DD}^2f\).

* Levers
| parameter       | how to reduce it                                           |
|-----------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| activity α      | fewer transitions: clock gating, encodings, glitch removal |
| capacitance C_L | smaller gates, shorter wires, fewer nodes                  |
| supply V_DD     | voltage scaling, multiple supplies (quadratic gain)        |
| frequency f     | lower clock: saves power, not energy per operation         |
| leakage I_leak  | power gating, high-threshold cells                         |

* Intuition
Every bit flip pays a toll proportional to the capacitance and to the square of the voltage. Low-power design is flipping fewer bits, smaller wires, at lower voltage.

* Pitfall
Halving \(f\) halves power but not energy: the task takes twice as long. Energy per operation \(\alpha C_LV_{DD}^2\) only falls by reducing \(\alpha\), \(C_L\) or \(V_{DD}\).
