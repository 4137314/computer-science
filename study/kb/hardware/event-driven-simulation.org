#+title: Event-driven logic simulation
#+summary: A logic simulator keeps a queue of future events ordered by time; it repeatedly takes the earliest events, updates the signals, evaluates only the gates whose inputs changed, and schedules their new outputs after their delays; the cost depends on how the queue is stored: linked list, balanced tree or timing wheel.

* Method
Types of simulation: compiled (evaluate every gate every cycle) or event-driven (evaluate only on change); zero-delay, unit-delay or real-delay. Only a small fraction of the circuit is active at any time, which event-driven simulation exploits.

* Algorithm
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require initial events in the queue $Q$
\While{$Q$ not empty and $t<t_{stop}$}
\State $t\gets$ time of the earliest event in $Q$
\State $E\gets$ all events at $t$; remove them from $Q$
\ForAll{$(s,v)\in E$ with $v\neq$ value of $s$}
\State set $s\gets v$; mark the fanout gates of $s$
\EndFor
\ForAll{marked gates $g$}
\State $v\gets$ evaluate $g$
\State insert $(\mathrm{out}(g),v)$ into $Q$ at time $t+d(g)$
\EndFor
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}

* Complexity
With \(n\) gates, simulated time \(T\) and activity proportional to \(n\) per time unit, about \(Tn\) events are processed. The cost of each queue insertion decides the total:
- *sorted linked list*: insertion \(O(n)\) (scan for the position): total \(O(Tn^2)\);
- *balanced tree* (heap): \(O(\log n)\): total \(O(Tn\log n)\);
- *timing wheel*: a circular array of buckets, one per time unit up to the maximum delay; inserting at \(t+d\) is indexing bucket \((t+d)\bmod W\): \(O(1)\), total \(O(Tn)\). Events beyond the wheel go to an overflow list.

* Intuition
Simulate only what changes. Most of a circuit is quiet most of the time, so following the events is far cheaper than recomputing everything.

* Pitfall
Event-driven simulation with real delays shows [[id:glitches][glitches]] that zero-delay functional simulation hides — and conversely a zero-delay simulation that passes says nothing about timing.
