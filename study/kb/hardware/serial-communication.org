#+title: Serial communication
#+summary: Sending bits one after another on few wires saves pins and avoids skew between parallel lines; synchronous links (SPI, I²C, I²S) share a clock wire, asynchronous ones (UART) agree on a bit rate and resynchronise on every character with a start bit.

* Definition
- *Parallel*: \(n\) data lines plus control; fast over short distances, but pins, cable and skew grow with \(n\).
- *Serial*: one or two data lines. *Synchronous*: the transmitter's clock is sent along (SCK, SCL): the receiver samples on its edges. *Asynchronous*: no clock; each side has its own, at a nominal rate; framing bits realign the receiver.
- *Duplex*: simplex (one direction), half duplex (both, alternately, e.g. I²C), full duplex (both at once, e.g. SPI, UART with TX and RX).
- *Topology*: point to point (UART), master with several slaves selected by wires (SPI) or by addresses on a shared bus (I²C).

|            | UART           | SPI              | I²C            | I²S         |
|------------+----------------+------------------+----------------+-------------|
| wires      | TX, RX         | SCK MOSI MISO SS | SCL, SDA       | SCK, WS, SD |
| clock      | none (agreed)  | master           | master         | master      |
| addressing | none           | SS per slave     | 7-bit address  | none        |
| duplex     | full           | full             | half           | one way     |
| speed      | ≤ 921.6 kbit/s | 10–50 Mbit/s     | 0.1–3.4 Mbit/s | audio       |

I²S carries stereo audio: the word-select line WS says whether the current word is the left or the right channel.

* Worked example
UART at 115 200 baud, 8N1: each byte costs 10 bit times (start + 8 data + stop): \(115\,200/10=11\,520\) bytes/s, 80% efficiency. SPI at 10 MHz moves 10 Mbit/s = 1.25 MB/s with no framing overhead. I²C at 400 kHz: 9 clocks per byte (8 data + ACK) plus address and start/stop per transaction: below 44 kB/s.

* Intuition
Serial links trade wires for time. With fast transistors and expensive pins, time is the cheaper resource.

* Pitfall
Two clock domains meet at every receiver: signals coming from outside must be synchronised (two flip-flops) before the receiver's FSM uses them, or [[id:setup-hold-time][metastability]] will eventually strike.
