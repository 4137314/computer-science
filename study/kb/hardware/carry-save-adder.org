#+title: Carry-save adders and adder trees
#+summary: A row of full adders with no carry chain reduces three \(n\)-bit operands to two (a sum word and a carry word shifted left) in the delay of one full adder; a tree of such 3:2 compressors reduces \(k\) operands to two in \(O(\log k)\) levels, and only the last addition needs a carry-propagate adder.

* Construction
For operands \(x,y,z\), bit \(i\): \(s_i=x_i\oplus y_i\oplus z_i\), \(c_{i+1}=\mathrm{maj}(x_i,y_i,z_i)\). Then
\[ x+y+z=S+2C, \]
with \(S=(s_i)\), \(C=(c_i)\): the sum is kept in *redundant* form (two words), and no carry propagates. Delay: one [[id:ripple-carry-adder][full adder]], independent of \(n\).

*Adder trees*: \(k\) operands need \(k-2\) CSA rows. In a chain they cost \(k-2\) full-adder delays; in a *Wallace tree* each level reduces the count by a factor \(3/2\), so \(\approx\log_{3/2}(k/2)\) levels. A final carry-propagate adder (ripple or lookahead) gives the result. A plain tree of carry-propagate adders would cost \(\lceil\log_2k\rceil\) full additions instead.

* Intuition
Postpone the carries: keep the result as "sum plus carries" while more numbers keep coming, and settle the carries once at the end.

* Pitfall
The redundant form \((S,C)\) cannot be compared or sign-tested directly; any decision on the value needs the final carry-propagate addition.
