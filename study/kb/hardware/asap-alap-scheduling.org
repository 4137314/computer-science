#+title: ASAP and ALAP scheduling, mobility
#+summary: Without resource limits, ASAP starts each operation as soon as its predecessors finish, giving the minimum latency \(\lambda\); ALAP starts it as late as possible within \(\lambda\); the mobility \(t^L_i-t^S_i\) of an operation says how much freedom it has, and zero-mobility operations form the critical path.

* Method
With \(d_i\) the execution delay (cycles) of operation \(v_i\):
- *ASAP*: \(t^S_{source}=1\); \(t^S_i=\max_{j\to i}\big(t^S_j+d_j\big)\), in topological order. The latency is \(\lambda=t^S_{sink}-1\).
- *ALAP* for a bound \(\bar\lambda\ge\lambda\): \(t^L_{sink}=\bar\lambda+1\); \(t^L_i=\min_{i\to k}\,t^L_k-d_i\), in reverse topological order (select a vertex whose successors are all scheduled).
- *Mobility*: \(\mu_i=t^L_i-t^S_i\ge0\). With \(\bar\lambda=\lambda\), operations with \(\mu_i=0\) are critical.
These are the arrival and required times of [[id:static-timing-analysis][timing analysis]], counted in cycles.

* Example
The [[id:data-flow-graph][solver graph]] with all delays 1, \(\bar\lambda=4\):

| op       | v1 | v2 | v3 | v4 | v5 | v6 | v7 | v8 | v9 | v10 | v11 |
|----------+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+-----+-----|
| ASAP     | 1  | 1  | 2  | 1  | 2  | 1  | 3  | 4  | 2  | 1   | 2   |
| ALAP     | 1  | 1  | 2  | 2  | 3  | 3  | 3  | 4  | 4  | 3   | 4   |
| mobility | 0  | 0  | 0  | 1  | 1  | 2  | 0  | 0  | 2  | 2   | 2   |

The critical path is v1, v2 → v3 → v7 → v8 (mobility 0). ASAP needs four multipliers in cycle 1; the mobility of v4, v5, v6 is exactly the freedom a [[id:list-scheduling][resource-constrained scheduler]] uses to spread them.

The addition can move from its ASAP cycle to its ALAP cycle without increasing the latency.

* Intuition
ASAP and ALAP are the two extremes; everything a real scheduler does happens between them.

* Pitfall
ASAP is optimal for latency only with unlimited resources: it puts all multiplications in the first cycle, which may need far more multipliers than the design can afford.
