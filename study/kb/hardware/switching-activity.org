#+title: Signal and transition probabilities
#+summary: If a node is 1 with probability \(p\) and successive cycles are independent, its \(0\to1\) transition probability is \(\alpha=(1-p)\,p\); output probabilities follow from input ones gate by gate (\(p_{AND}=p_Ap_B\), \(p_{OR}=1-(1-p_A)(1-p_B)\)) as long as the inputs are independent, which reconvergent fanout breaks.

* Method
Static probability \(p=P(x=1)\). With temporal independence,
\[ \alpha_{0\to1}=P(x_{t-1}=0)\,P(x_t=1)=(1-p)\,p,\]
maximal (\(1/4\)) at \(p=1/2\). For independent inputs \(A\), \(B\):

| gate | p_out (independent inputs) | α 0→1     | with p_A = p_B = 1/2 |
|------+----------------------------+-----------+----------------------|
| AND  | p_A·p_B                    | (1 − p)·p | p = 1/4, α = 3/16    |
| OR   | 1 − (1 − p_A)(1 − p_B)     | (1 − p)·p | p = 3/4, α = 3/16    |
| NAND | 1 − p_A·p_B                | (1 − p)·p | p = 3/4, α = 3/16    |
| XOR  | p_A + p_B − 2·p_A·p_B      | (1 − p)·p | p = 1/2, α = 1/4     |

With \(p_A=p_B=\tfrac12\): NAND has \(p=\tfrac34\), \(\alpha=\tfrac3{16}\); AND \(p=\tfrac14\), \(\alpha=\tfrac3{16}\); XOR \(p=\tfrac12\), \(\alpha=\tfrac14\).

* Correlations
- *Spatial*: reconvergent fanout makes gate inputs dependent. For \(y=x\cdot\bar x\) the formula gives \(p(1-p)\), but \(y\equiv0\). Exact computation conditions on the reconvergent signals (via [[id:boolean-difference][cofactors]]) or uses BDDs.
- *Temporal*: real signals are correlated in time (counters, slowly varying data), so \(\alpha\neq p(1-p)\); one uses transition probabilities \(p_{01}\) directly.

Activity is highest for signals that are 1 half of the time.

* Intuition
A node that is almost always 0 or almost always 1 rarely switches; balanced, random signals switch the most.

* Pitfall
Propagating probabilities gate by gate ignores glitches: the zero-delay estimate underestimates the real activity of deep, unbalanced logic.
