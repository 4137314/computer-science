#+title: Full adder and ripple-carry adder
#+summary: A full adder computes \(s=a\oplus b\oplus c\) and \(c_{out}=ab+c(a\oplus b)\); chaining \(n\) of them adds \(n\)-bit numbers with delay \(O(n)\), because the carry ripples through every stage; in two's complement the same circuit subtracts (\(a-b=a+\bar b+1\)).

* Construction
Per bit \(i\): *generate* \(g_i=a_ib_i\) (a carry is born), *propagate* \(p_i=a_i\oplus b_i\) (an incoming carry passes),
\[ s_i=p_i\oplus c_i,\qquad c_{i+1}=g_i+p_i\,c_i. \]
\(n\) full adders in a chain, \(c_0=c_{in}\). Area \(O(n)\), delay \(\approx n\,t_{carry}\).

Two's complement: an \(n\)-bit word represents \(-a_{n-1}2^{n-1}+\sum_{i<n-1}a_i2^i\). Subtraction \(a-b=a+\bar b+1\): invert \(b\), set \(c_0=1\). Overflow (signed) when \(c_n\ne c_{n-1}\).

The carry (amber) crosses every stage: the critical path is linear in \(n\).

* Intuition
Adding by hand, column by column, with the carry passed to the left: simple, small, and as slow as the number is long.

* Pitfall
The worst case (\(p_i=1\) in every position, e.g. \(0111\dots1+0000\dots1\)) is rare for random data but sets the clock period, since timing analysis must assume it.
