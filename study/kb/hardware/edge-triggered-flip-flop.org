#+title: Edge-triggered flip-flops: master–slave
#+summary: A latch in a feedback loop is transparent for the whole active clock phase, so the state can change several times per cycle (the circuit oscillates, at a rate set by its delays); two latches clocked in opposition — master open while the clock is high, slave open while it is low — are never transparent end to end, so the output changes once per clock edge and takes the value \(D\) had just before that edge.

* Construction
Why a latch is not enough: in a synchronous loop (next-state logic → latch → next-state logic), while \(C=1\) the latch is transparent; a fast enough logic delivers the new next state before \(C\) falls and the state changes again. Example: \(D=\bar Q\) through a wire — with \(C=1\) the latch oscillates, with \(C=0\) it stops on an unpredictable value. Narrowing the active pulse below the minimum logic delay works on paper but needs very steep, short pulses on a heavily loaded clock net: big drivers, energy, electromagnetic noise.
*Master–slave*: \(D\to\) master latch (enabled by \(C\)) \(\to Q_M\to\) slave latch (enabled by \(\bar C\)) \(\to Q\).
- \(C=1\): master transparent, \(Q_M\) follows \(D\); slave closed, \(Q\) still.
- \(C:1\to0\): master closes on the current \(D\); slave opens and copies \(Q_M\) to \(Q\).
- \(C=0\): master closed, slave sees a constant input: nothing moves.
There is never a transparent path from \(D\) to \(Q\). This one is *negative-edge triggered*; with the enables swapped it is *positive-edge triggered* (the usual \(\mathtt{rising\_edge}\) register; drawn with a triangle on \(C\), a bubble for the falling edge).
*Sampling semantics* (the important part): the value of \(D\) just before the active edge is found on \(Q\) just after it. Between edges \(D\) may do anything — even glitch — provided it is stable in the [[id:setup-hold-time][setup/hold window]] around the edge. Hence the next-state logic only has to settle within one clock period, and combinational [[id:logic-hazards][hazards]] do not propagate.
*Preset and clear*: asynchronous inputs (often active low, \(\bar S\), \(\bar R\)) that force \(Q=1\) or \(Q=0\) immediately, regardless of the clock — used to start from a known state at power-up.

* Example
Same \(D\) waveform into a D *latch* (transparent while \(C=1\)): during the high phase 1–3 the glitch at 2–2.4 passes straight to \(Q\); the flip-flop above ignores it. That is the practical difference between level and edge sensitivity.

* Intuition
A pair of airlock doors: one is always shut, so nothing can run straight through; the data moves one chamber per clock half-period.

* Pitfall
"Edge-triggered" does not mean "infinitely fast": violating setup or hold makes the internal loop resolve late and possibly at random ([[id:metastability][metastability]]). And the asynchronous clear acts *at once*, not at the next edge — a common timing-diagram mistake.
