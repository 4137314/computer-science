#+title: Timing diagrams and static hazards
#+summary: With gate delays a correct two-level circuit can glitch: when an input change moves the input point from one implicant to another and the first switches off before the second switches on, an output that should stay 1 drops briefly (static-1 hazard); on the Karnaugh map it is a jump between adjacent ones not covered by a common group, and adding the bridging consensus implicant removes it at the cost of minimality.

* Definition
A *timing diagram* plots every node against time, with two levels only. Without delays it checks the function; with delays it shows the transient. Procedure (event-driven, as a [[id:event-driven-simulation][simulator]] does):
1. inputs constant for \(t<0\); compute every node at \(t=0\) (steady state);
2. move to the earliest time at which some signal changes (an *event*);
3. re-evaluate the gates fed by that signal and schedule their output changes after their delay;
4. repeat until no events remain.
Delay from input to output depends on the path: through a 2-to-1 [[id:multiplexer][mux]] a change on \(a\) takes \(2\Delta\), one on \(s\) takes \(3\Delta\) (inverter + AND + OR).
*Hazard (alea, glitch)*: a short spurious pulse during the transient. *Static-1*: output should stay 1 and drops; *static-0*: should stay 0 and rises; *dynamic*: should change once and changes three times.

\(a=b=1\), \(s\) falls at \(t=1\), every gate has delay \(\Delta=1\): \(sb\) dies at 2, \(\bar sa\) is born only at 3, so \(f\) drops from 3 to 4.

* Example
2-to-1 mux, \(f=\bar sa+sb\); map rows \(s\), columns \(ab\):

| s/ab | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10 |
|------+----+----+----+----|
| 0    |  0 |  0 |  1 |  1 |
| 1    |  0 |  1 |  1 |  0 |

With \(a=b=1\) and \(s\) switching, the input point moves between cells \(011\) and \(111\): both ones, adjacent, but in different groups (\(\bar sa\), \(sb\)). The fix is the *bridging* (consensus) implicant \(ab\): \(f=\bar sa+sb+ab\) stays 1 through the transition because \(ab\) does not depend on \(s\). The \(s:0\to1\) transition does not glitch with these delays (\(sb\) rises after \(\Delta\), \(\bar sa\) falls after \(2\Delta\)), but a hazard-free design must cover both directions.
Rule: an SOP is free of static-1 hazards for single-input changes iff every pair of adjacent ones is covered by a common implicant. Dually, static-0 hazards in a POS are removed by covering adjacent zeros together.

* Intuition
Two implicants hand the output over like runners passing a baton; if the first lets go before the second grabs, the baton falls. The consensus term is a third runner holding it during the handover.

* Pitfall
In a synchronous circuit glitches on combinational nodes are harmless for function: an [[id:edge-triggered-flip-flop][edge-triggered flip-flop]] samples only at the edge, after the transient — they cost [[id:glitches][power]]. They matter where a signal is used as a clock, an asynchronous reset or in an asynchronous sequential circuit. Hazards caused by several inputs changing together cannot all be removed by adding terms: in an adder the carry chain makes them unavoidable.
