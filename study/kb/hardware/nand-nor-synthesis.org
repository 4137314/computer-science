#+title: NAND–NAND and NOR–NOR realizations
#+summary: Applying De Morgan twice, a minimal SOP \(f=\sum p_i\) becomes \(f=\overline{\prod\overline{p_i}}\): every AND and the final OR turn into NANDs with the same implicants; dually a minimal POS becomes a NOR–NOR network; single literals entering the output gate directly must be complemented.

* Construction
From SOP to NAND–NAND:
\[ f=p_1+p_2+\dots+p_k=\overline{\overline{p_1+\dots+p_k}}=\overline{\bar p_1\,\bar p_2\cdots\bar p_k}. \]
Each \(\bar p_i\) is a NAND of the literals of \(p_i\); the outer bar-of-product is a NAND. Procedure: replace every AND by a NAND, the OR by a NAND, and complement a literal that goes straight into the output gate (a one-literal term \(z\) enters as \(\bar z\), since \(\bar p_i=\bar z\)).
From POS to NOR–NOR: \(f=\prod s_i=\overline{\sum\bar s_i}\), each \(\bar s_i\) a NOR of the literals of \(s_i\); implicants come from the *zeros* of the map.
The implicants are exactly those of the AND–OR (or OR–AND) form: the map work does not change.

* Example
\(f=z+\bar y\bar w+yw+x\bar z\):
\[ f=\overline{\bar z\cdot\overline{\bar y\bar w}\cdot\overline{yw}\cdot\overline{x\bar z}} \]
three 2-input NANDs (\(\bar y\bar w\), \(yw\), \(x\bar z\)), the literal \(z\) complemented to \(\bar z\), and one 4-input NAND at the output: two levels, one gate type.

* Intuition
A NAND is an AND followed by an inverter, and also an OR with inverted inputs; between two levels the two inversions annihilate.

* Pitfall
Forgetting to invert the single literals feeding the output NAND gives \(\bar z\) where \(z\) was meant. In CMOS, inverting gates are cheaper (4 transistors for a 2-input NAND, 6 for an AND), so NAND–NAND is usually smaller and faster than AND–OR, not just "more uniform".
