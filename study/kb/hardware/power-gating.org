#+title: Power gating and leakage reduction
#+summary: Clock gating stops dynamic power but not leakage; power gating disconnects an idle block from the supply with a high-threshold sleep transistor (header or footer), cutting leakage by orders of magnitude, at the cost of wake-up time and energy, isolation cells on the outputs and retention registers for state that must survive.

* Construction
- *Sleep transistor*: a wide high-\(V_T\) PMOS between \(V_{DD}\) and the block's [[id:virtual-functions][virtual]] supply (header) or NMOS to ground (footer). Off: [[id:cmos-power][leakage]] limited by the high-\(V_T\) device. On: small series resistance, slight speed loss.
- *Multi-threshold CMOS*: low-\(V_T\) (fast, leaky) cells on critical paths, high-\(V_T\) cells elsewhere.
- *Isolation cells*: clamp the outputs of a powered-down block to known values so that floating nodes do not cause short-circuit current in powered blocks.
- *Retention registers*: a small always-on latch saves the state before shutdown.
- *Break-even time*: gating pays only if the block stays off longer than \(E_{wake}/P_{leak}\), the time in which saved leakage equals the energy spent turning off and on.

* Derivation
Subthreshold leakage \(I_{leak}\propto e^{-V_T/(n\,kT/q)}\): with \(n\,kT/q\approx40\) mV, raising \(V_T\) by 100 mV reduces leakage by about \(e^{2.5}\approx12\), by 300 mV about \(1800\). This is why a single high-\(V_T\) switch in series controls the leakage of thousands of low-\(V_T\) gates.

* Intuition
[[id:clock-gating][Clock gating]] is putting a machine on pause; power gating is unplugging it — far more saving, but restarting takes time and you lose what was in its memory unless you saved it.

* Pitfall
Turning a large block on draws a current surge (in-rush) that disturbs neighbouring blocks' supply; switches are turned on gradually, which adds wake-up latency.
