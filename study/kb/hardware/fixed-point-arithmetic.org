#+title: Fixed-point arithmetic
#+summary: Hardware DSP stores real numbers as integers with an implicit binary point: in Qm.n a word of \(m+n+1\) bits represents \(x=\text{integer}\cdot2^{-n}\); addition aligns points, multiplication adds fraction bits (Qa.b × Qc.d → Q(a+c+1).(b+d)), and every step must choose rounding versus truncation and saturation versus wrap-around.

* Definition
Signed Qm.n: 1 sign bit, \(m\) integer bits, \(n\) fraction bits. Range \([-2^m,\,2^m-2^{-n}]\), resolution \(2^{-n}\).
- *Addition*: same format in, one extra integer bit out to avoid overflow.
- *Multiplication*: Q\(a\).\(b\) × Q\(c\).\(d\) = Q\((a+c+1)\).\((b+d)\); the product is then shifted right by the excess fraction bits.
- *Quantization error*: truncation (drop bits) has bias \(-2^{-n-1}\) on average; rounding to nearest has zero mean and error uniform in \(\pm2^{-n-1}\), variance \(2^{-2n}/12\).
- *Overflow*: wrap-around (two's complement default) gives huge errors; saturation clips to the extremes.

* Example
Q1.6 (8 bits): \(0.75=48\cdot2^{-6}\) is =00110000=, \(-0.5\) is =11100000=. Product: \(48\cdot(-32)=-1536\) in Q3.12; shift right by 6: \(-24\) in Q1.6, i.e. \(-24/64=-0.375\) ✓.
#+begin_src vhdl
  signal a, b : signed(7 downto 0);              -- Q1.6
  signal p    : signed(15 downto 0);             -- Q3.12
  p <= a * b;
  y <= resize(shift_right(p + 32, 6), 8);         -- round to nearest, back to Q1.6
#+end_src

* Intuition
Fixed point is doing all your sums in, say, cents: integers everywhere, and you just remember where the decimal point is.

* Pitfall
Word lengths grow at every multiplication and accumulation (a FIR with \(N\) taps needs \(\lceil\log_2N\rceil\) guard bits in the accumulator); forgetting them gives silent wrap-around, the worst possible error.
