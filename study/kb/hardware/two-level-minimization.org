#+title: Two-level minimization: prime implicants and covers
#+summary: A minimum sum of products is a cover of the on-set by prime implicants (cubes that cannot be enlarged without hitting the off-set); Quine–McCluskey generates all primes by merging cubes differing in one variable, essential primes are taken first, and the rest is a set-cover problem — NP-hard in general, solved heuristically by Espresso.

* Method
1. *Implicant*: a cube \(c\) (product of literals) with \(c\Rightarrow f\). *Prime*: no literal can be removed keeping \(c\Rightarrow f\). Every minimum SOP uses only primes.
2. *Generate primes* (Quine–McCluskey): list minterms (and don't-cares) by number of ones; merge pairs differing in exactly one bit (\(x\bar y z+xyz=xz\)); repeat on the merged cubes; cubes never merged are prime.
3. *Prime implicant chart*: rows = primes, columns = minterms (not don't-cares). A minterm covered by a single prime makes that prime *essential*.
4. *Cover* the remaining minterms with the fewest (cheapest) primes: row/column dominance, then branching (Petrick's method) — exponential in the worst case.
A Karnaugh map does the same by eye for up to 4–5 variables: largest power-of-two rectangles of ones, wrapping at the edges.

* Example
\(f(a,b,c)=\sum m(0,1,2,5,6,7)\). Merging: \(000+001=\bar a\bar b\), \(000+010=\bar a\bar c\), \(001+101=\bar bc\), \(010+110=b\bar c\), \(101+111=ac\), \(110+111=ab\). No further merges: six primes. Every minterm is covered by two primes, none essential (a cyclic cover). Minimum covers have three primes: \(f=\bar a\bar b+b\bar c+ac\) or \(f=\bar a\bar c+\bar bc+ab\).

* Intuition
Make every product as large as possible (prime), then pick the fewest large products that together touch every one.

* Pitfall
Greedy "take the biggest prime first" is not optimal on cyclic charts; and two-level minimality says nothing about multilevel area or delay — it is only the first step of synthesis.
