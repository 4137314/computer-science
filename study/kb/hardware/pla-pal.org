#+title: PLA and PAL
#+summary: Two-level programmable logic: a programmable AND plane forms product terms of the inputs and their complements, an OR plane sums them into the outputs; a PLA programs both planes, a PAL only the AND plane with a fixed number of products per output.

* Construction
Every input enters the AND plane in true and complemented form. Each AND line computes a product term (a cube); each output is the OR of selected products.
- *PROM*: AND plane fixed (all \(2^n\) minterms), OR plane programmable — the [[id:rom-as-logic][memory]] case.
- *PLA*: both planes programmable. Products are shared among outputs, so the number of AND lines is the number of distinct cubes of a minimised multi-output cover. Slower (two programmable planes).
- *PAL*: AND plane programmable, OR plane fixed — each output ORs a fixed group of, say, 7 products. Faster and cheaper; products cannot be shared. Outputs fed back as inputs implement multilevel functions, at the cost of extra delay through the array; registered outputs make sequential circuits (GAL, 22V10).
- *CPLD*: several PAL-like blocks connected by a programmable interconnect.

\(P_0=x_0\bar x_1\), \(P_1=\bar x_0x_1\), \(P_2=x_0x_1\); \(f_0=P_0+P_2\), \(f_1=P_1+P_2\): \(P_2\) is shared, which a PLA allows.

* Example
A [[id:ripple-carry-adder][full adder]]: \(s=a\oplus b\oplus c\) needs four products (its four odd minterms), \(c_{out}=ab+ac+bc\) three. A PLA with 7 AND lines and 2 outputs [[id:abstract-class-interface][implements]] it; a PAL needs at least 4 products per output.

* Intuition
A PLA is a minimised truth table in hardware: only the product terms you need, wired to the outputs that need them.

* Pitfall
Two-level forms of some functions are huge: the [[id:uart][parity]] of \(n\) bits needs \(2^{n-1}\) products. Such functions belong in multilevel logic.
