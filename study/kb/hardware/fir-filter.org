#+title: FIR filter architectures
#+summary: \(y[n]=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}h_k\,x[n-k]\): in direct form a delay line feeds \(N\) multipliers and a chain of adders (critical path one multiplier plus \(N-1\) adders); retiming the delays across the adders gives the transposed form, critical path one multiplier and one adder; a serial version uses one MAC for \(N\) cycles.

* Construction
- *Direct form*: registers \(x[n],x[n-1],\dots\) (tapped delay line), products \(h_kx[n-k]\), adder chain. [[id:static-timing-analysis][Critical path]] \(T_{mul}+(N-1)T_{add}\).
- *Direct pipelined*: registers in the adder chain (and matching ones on the delay line) cut the path to \(T_{mul}+T_{add}\), adding latency.
- *Transposed form*: the input \(x[n]\) is broadcast to all multipliers and the registers sit between the adders: \(z_k[n]=h_kx[n]+z_{k+1}[n-1]\), \(y[n]=z_0[n]\). Critical path \(T_{mul}+T_{add}\) with no extra latency. It is obtained from the direct form by [[id:retiming][retiming]]: reverse the order of additions, move the delays across the adders, remove the addition with 0.
- *Serial*: one multiply–accumulate unit, a coefficient ROM and a [[id:stack-and-queue][circular buffer]]: \(N\) cycles per output, minimal area.
- *[[id:carry-save-adder][Adder tree]] / CSA*: the \(N\) products summed in carry-save form.

Transposed form: the input is broadcast, the registers sit in the accumulation chain, each adder sees one product and one register.

* Evaluation
Compare architectures on area (multipliers, adders, registers), throughput (outputs per second = \(1/T\) or \(1/(NT)\)), latency and power (switching in the adder chain, broadcast load on \(x[n]\)). Constant coefficients allow shift-and-add multipliers (canonical signed digits).

* Intuition
The same equation fits a long fast pipeline or one small unit reused \(N\) times; retiming moves between equivalent shapes for free.

* Pitfall
In the transposed form the input drives \(N\) multipliers: the fan-out load on \(x[n]\) grows with \(N\) and must be buffered.
