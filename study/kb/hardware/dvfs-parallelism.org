#+title: Voltage scaling, DVFS and parallel datapaths
#+summary: Lowering \(V_{DD}\) cuts energy quadratically but slows the gates; parallel or pipelined datapaths recover the throughput at the lower voltage, so a duplicated unit at half frequency and reduced voltage can do the same work for roughly 40% of the power; DVFS adapts voltage and frequency to the current load.

* Method
[[id:gate-delay-model][Gate delay]] grows as the supply approaches the threshold, roughly \(d\propto V_{DD}/(V_{DD}-V_T)^2\). Strategies:
- *Multiple supplies*: critical paths at high \(V_{DD}\), the rest at low \(V_{DD}\) (voltage islands, level shifters between them).
- *DVFS*: when the workload is light, lower \(f\) and then \(V_{DD}\) to the minimum that meets the new period. Since \(P\propto V^2f\), power falls roughly with the cube of the scaling factor, [[id:cmos-power][energy per operation]] with the square.
- *Parallelism*: two copies of a unit, each at \(f/2\), deliver the same throughput; with twice the time per operation each copy can run at a lower voltage.
- *Pipelining*: shorter stages tolerate slower gates at the same frequency, so \(V_{DD}\) can drop.

* Derivation
Reference: one unit, capacitance \(C\), voltage \(V\), frequency \(f\): \(P_{ref}=CV^2f\). Parallel version: two units plus a [[id:vhdl-concurrent-assignments][multiplexer]], capacitance \(\approx2.2C\), frequency \(f/2\), and the doubled delay budget allows \(V'\approx0.6V\):
\[ P_{par}=2.2C\,(0.6V)^2\,\frac f2\approx0.4\,P_{ref}. \]
Area roughly doubles: power is traded for silicon.

* Intuition
Two slow, relaxed workers use less energy than one fast, stressed worker, because speed costs voltage and voltage costs its square.

* Pitfall
Below a certain voltage leakage dominates and the slower circuit leaks for longer: energy per operation has a minimum, and scaling further makes things worse.
