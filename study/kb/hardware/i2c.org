#+title: I²C
#+summary: A two-wire, half-duplex, multi-device bus: SDA (data) and SCL (clock) are open-drain lines with pull-ups, so any device can pull them low; SDA changes only while SCL is low, except for START (SDA falls with SCL high) and STOP (SDA rises with SCL high); each byte is followed by an acknowledge bit driven by the receiver.

* Construction
Transaction:
1. *START*: SDA \(1\to0\) while SCL = 1.
2. *Address*: 7 bits, MSB first, plus R/\(\overline{W}\) (1 = read).
3. *ACK*: the master releases SDA during the ninth clock; the addressed slave pulls it to 0.
4. *Data bytes*: 8 bits + ACK each; when reading, the master acknowledges each byte and sends NACK (SDA left high) after the last.
5. *STOP*: SDA \(0\to1\) while SCL = 1. A *repeated START* instead of STOP keeps the bus to switch direction (write a register address, then read).
Slaves can hold SCL low to slow the master (clock stretching).

* VHDL: open-drain lines
#+begin_src vhdl
  -- drive only 0, release with 'Z'; the external pull-up makes it 'H'
  sda <= '0' when sda_drive_low = '1' else 'Z';
  scl <= '0' when scl_drive_low = '1' else 'Z';
  sda_in <= to_X01(sda);        -- read back the resolved line ('H' -> '1')
#+end_src
In a testbench, =sda <= 'H';= models the pull-up; [[id:std-logic][resolution]] gives =0= when a device drives low.

START and STOP are the only SDA edges allowed while SCL is high.

* Intuition
A party line: everyone listens, anyone can say "no" by pulling the wire down, and the address decides who answers.

* Pitfall
Driving SDA or SCL high actively (='1'= instead of ='Z'=) fights the device pulling low: a short circuit on real hardware and an =X= in simulation.
