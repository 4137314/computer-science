#+title: Multipliers
#+summary: An \(n\times n\) multiplication sums \(n\) partial products \(a\cdot b_j\,2^j\), each a row of AND gates; an array of carry-save adders reduces them in \(O(n)\) delay, a Wallace tree in \(O(\log n)\), and Booth recoding halves the number of rows; a sequential shift-and-add multiplier uses one adder for \(n\) cycles.

* Construction
\[ a\cdot b=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} b_j\,a\,2^{j},\qquad pp_{j,i}=a_i\wedge b_j . \]
- *Array multiplier*: \(n\) rows of AND gates, \(n-1\) rows of adders, each row adding the next partial product to the running sum in carry-save form; regular layout, delay \(\approx 2n\) full adders. Area \(O(n^2)\).
- *Tree multiplier*: all partial products at once into a [[id:carry-save-adder][Wallace or Dadda tree]], \(O(\log n)\) levels, then a fast carry-propagate adder.
- *Radix-4 Booth*: recode \(b\) into digits in \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\) by looking at overlapping bit triples; \(n/2\) partial products, each a shifted or negated copy of \(a\). It also handles two's complement multipliers directly.
- *Sequential*: one adder and a shift register: \(n\) cycles, \(O(n)\) area.

* Intuition
School multiplication in binary is easy — each row is \(a\) or zero — and the whole difficulty is adding the rows fast.

* Pitfall
Signed operands need care: two's complement partial products must be sign-extended (or use Baugh–Wooley/Booth), otherwise the high half of the product is wrong.
