#+title: Iterative magnitude comparator
#+summary: Comparing two \(n\)-bit unsigned numbers bit by bit from the most significant end, each cell passes "already greater / already less" downwards and decides only if everything above was equal: \(G_i=G_{i+1}+E_{i+1}a_i\bar b_i\), \(L_i=L_{i+1}+E_{i+1}\bar a_ib_i\), \(E=\overline{G+L}\); the structure is the ripple-carry adder's, with the same linear delay and the same look-ahead tricks.

* Construction
*Half comparator* (one bit): \(g=a\bar b\) (\(a>b\)), \(l=\bar ab\) (\(a<b\)), \(e=\overline{a\oplus b}\) (equal). Three cases, two output bits suffice (\(e=\bar g\bar l\)).
*Iterative cell* \(i\), fed by the cell above (\(i+1\)) with \(G_n=L_n=0\):
\[ G_i=G_{i+1}+\overline{G_{i+1}+L_{i+1}}\;a_i\bar b_i,\qquad L_i=L_{i+1}+\overline{G_{i+1}+L_{i+1}}\;\bar a_ib_i. \]
Outputs at the end: \(A>B=G_0\), \(A<B=L_0\), \(A=B=\overline{G_0+L_0}\). A decision taken by a higher bit is never overridden, like a carry that is generated and then propagated.
The LSB-first version is the adder's carry chain: \(\mathit{gt}_{i+1}=a_i\bar b_i+\overline{a_i\oplus b_i}\,\mathit{gt}_i\) — "greater so far" is generated by \(a_i\bar b_i\) and propagated when the bits are equal.
Alternatives: \(A<B\) is the borrow of \(A-B\), i.e. \(\overline{c_n}\) of \(A+\bar B+1\) on an existing [[id:ripple-carry-adder][adder]]; equality alone is a tree of XNORs and an AND.

* Example
\(A=1011\), \(B=1001\). Bit 3: \(1,1\) equal, \(G=L=0\). Bit 2: \(0,0\) equal. Bit 1: \(1,0\): \(G_1=1\). Bit 0: \(1,1\), but \(G\) is already 1 and stays: \(A>B\).
BCD comparators work digit by digit in the same way, each cell comparing two 4-bit digits.

* Intuition
Reading two numbers from the left, the first position where they differ decides; everything after it is irrelevant.

* Pitfall
For two's-complement operands the sign bit has the opposite weight: compare the MSBs inverted (or flip both sign bits) before using the unsigned comparator.
