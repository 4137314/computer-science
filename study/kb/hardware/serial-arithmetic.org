#+title: Bit-serial arithmetic
#+summary: A serial adder processes one bit per clock, least significant first, with one full adder and a flip-flop holding the carry: \(n\) cycles per \(n\)-bit addition, constant area; serial and digit-serial units trade throughput for area and wiring, and fit serial data streams.

* Construction
Shift registers present \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) at cycle \(i\); a full adder computes \(s_i\) and \(c_{i+1}\); a D flip-flop stores \(c_{i+1}\) for the next cycle (reset to \(c_0\) at the start of each word). Sum bits are shifted out. Area: one full adder and one flip-flop, independent of \(n\); time: \(n\) cycles, but each cycle is a single full-adder delay, so the clock can be fast.

*Digit-serial*: process \(d\) bits per cycle with a \(d\)-bit adder: \(n/d\) cycles. Serial multipliers combine a serial operand with a parallel one (\(n\) cycles, \(n\) adders).

* Intuition
The ripple-carry adder unrolled in time instead of space: the carry waits in a flip-flop instead of travelling along a wire.

* Pitfall
Serial units need control (word boundaries, carry reset) and conversion to and from parallel form; for short words the overhead eats the area saving.
