#+title: How a hash table works (HashMap)
#+summary: A hash table is an array of \(m\) buckets; key \(k\) goes to bucket \(h(k)\bmod m\), collisions are chained in the bucket; with load factor \(\alpha=n/m\) bounded (0.75 in =HashMap=), lookups cost \(O(1+\alpha)\) on average, and when \(\alpha\) is exceeded the table doubles and every entry is rehashed — \(O(1)\) amortised per insertion.

* Construction
- *Index*: =HashMap= mixes the high bits into the low ones, \(h\oplus(h\gg16)\), then takes =h & (m-1)= with \(m\) a power of two.
- *Collisions*: separate chaining (list per bucket); since Java 8 a bucket with more than 8 entries becomes a balanced tree, bounding the worst case at \(O(\log n)\) for [[id:comparable-comparator][comparable]] keys.
- *Lookup*: find the bucket, then compare hashes and call =equals= on each entry of the bucket.
- *Resize*: when \(n>0.75\,m\), allocate \(2m\) buckets and reinsert every entry.

* Derivation
With uniform hashing a bucket holds on average \(\alpha\) entries, so an unsuccessful search inspects \(\alpha\) of them and a successful one about \(1+\alpha/2\). Doubling at \(n=\alpha_{max}m\) costs \(O(n)\), but it happens after \(n/2\) insertions since the previous doubling; spreading the cost gives a constant per insertion: \(1+2+4+\dots+n<2n\) total moves for \(n\) insertions.

* Intuition
Hashing turns "search the whole list" into "go straight to the right drawer and look through the few items in it".

* Pitfall
A poor =hashCode= (constant, or using few fields) puts everything in one bucket and turns \(O(1)\) into \(O(n)\); and iteration order of a =HashMap= is not insertion order — use =LinkedHashMap= when it matters.
